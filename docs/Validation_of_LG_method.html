Validation of the A4 (auxiliary-problem) condition in the certified Lehmann–Goerisch computation for H2+

Scope: how the auxiliary/A4 condition of the Lehmann–Goerisch (LG) lower-bound method is handled rigorously in the present interval-arithmetic computation, given that the operator contains non-separable Coulomb terms $1/|x-a_i|$. The report states the exact algorithm implemented in lg_verified.jl / lg_oee.jl / dirichlet_lg.jl, the identity that replaces the exact A4 solve, and the resulting certificates.

One-sentence answer. The A4 auxiliary problem is never solved exactly — with the Coulomb term that is impossible in a finite basis. Instead we use the Goerisch defect identity: solve the auxiliary system approximately, then rigorously enclose the missing component of $w_i$ (the exact "shift part" the auxiliary solution still needs) by a one-sided interval $[0,\,\lVert r\rVert^2/\lambda_{\min}]$. Because the LG bound is monotone in the resulting quantity $A_2$, the enclosed $A_2$ yields a rigorous lower bound.

1. The A4 condition and why it cannot be enforced exactly here

Write the (shifted, symmetric positive-definite) operator as $H' = H + cI$ on the finite Galerkin space $V\subset H^1$ (or $H^1_0$ for the Dirichlet sectors), with $c>0$ chosen so that $H'$ is SPD. The single-test-vector LG bound needs, for the normalized test vector $v$, the auxiliary vector

$$ w \;=\; H'^{-1} v, \qquad\text{i.e. the exact solution of}\qquad b\,(H'\,w,\,\phi)=\langle v,\phi\rangle \;\;\forall \phi\in V. $$

This is exactly the A4 statement (“given $v_i$, find $w_i$ with $b(Tw_i,v)=N(v_i,v)$ for all $v$”): $T$ is the solution operator of $H'$, and $w=Tv$. The quantity the LG certificate ultimately consumes is

$$ A_2 \;=\; \langle v,\;H'^{-1} v\rangle \;=\; \langle v, w\rangle . $$
Why exactness fails. $H'$ contains the Coulomb potential $-\sum_i 1/|x-a_i|$, represented in the cosine/sine basis by a dense interval moment matrix (every basis pair couples through the Laplace representation $1/r=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-r^2 t^2}\,dt$). There is no closed form for $H'^{-1}v$ in the basis, and no finite exact arithmetic that inverts a $D\times D$ dense interval matrix ($D$ up to $68{,}921$ here). So $w$ can only be computed approximately, and A4 cannot hold exactly.

2. The algorithm actually used: Goerisch defect identity

Let $\tilde w$ be an approximate solution of $H'w=v$, obtained by conjugate gradients on the floating-point midpoint matrix $H'_{\mathrm{mid}}=\mathrm{mid}(H')$. Define the interval residual

$$ r \;=\; v - H'\,\tilde w \qquad(\text{computed with the full interval } H'). $$

The exact value we need obeys the algebraic identity, valid for any SPD $M=H'$ and any $\tilde w$:

$$ \boxed{\;\langle v,\,H'^{-1}v\rangle \;=\; 2\langle \tilde w, v\rangle \;-\; \langle \tilde w, H'\tilde w\rangle \;+\; \langle r,\,H'^{-1} r\rangle\;} \tag{$\star$}$$

Derivation: expand the last term with $r=v-H'\tilde w$ and use self-adjointness, $\langle H'\tilde w, H'^{-1}v\rangle=\langle \tilde w,v\rangle$; the $\tilde w$-terms cancel and the identity is exact. Everything except $\langle r,H'^{-1}r\rangle$ is directly computable in interval arithmetic.

The single non-computable term is the defect energy $\langle r, H'^{-1} r\rangle$. It is exactly the contribution of the still-missing part of $w$ — the component $e = H'^{-1}r = H'^{-1}v-\tilde w = w-\tilde w$, i.e. “the part of $w_i$ that a final exact stage would add.” We do not compute $e$; we bound its energy rigorously by the two-sided spectral estimate

$$ 0 \;\le\; \langle r,\,H'^{-1} r\rangle \;\le\; \frac{\lVert r\rVert^2}{\lambda_{\min}(H')} , \tag{$\dagger$}$$

giving the certified enclosure

$$ A_2 \;\in\; 2\langle \tilde w, v\rangle - \langle \tilde w, H'\tilde w\rangle \;+\; \Big[\,0,\ \frac{\lVert r\rVert^2}{\lambda_{\min}(H')}\,\Big]. $$
This is precisely the “determine the shift part at the final stage” technique, in bounded form. Rather than constructing the exact correction $e=H'^{-1}r$ (which would require the exact solve we cannot do), we place its entire energy contribution inside the one-sided interval $[0,\lVert r\rVert^2/\lambda_{\min}]$. The lower endpoint $0$ is exact (the term is a norm, $\langle r,H'^{-1}r\rangle\ge 0$ since $H'$ is SPD); the upper endpoint uses the Rayleigh bound $H'^{-1}\preceq \lambda_{\min}(H')^{-1} I$.

2.1 Corresponding code

Hc   = form_H(P, kin; sigma = interval(c))          # H' = H + cI   (SPD)
Hms  = mid.(Hc)                                      # midpoint (float) matrix
cg!(w, Symmetric(Hms), v; reltol=1e-12, maxiter=8000)  # approximate solve H'_mid w = v
Hv   = imatvec(Hc, vI);  A0 = idot(vI, Hv)           # A0 = <v, H'v>
wI   = interval.(w);  Hw = imatvec(Hc, wI)           # interval H' * w
r    = vI .- Hw                                       # interval residual r = v - H'w
wv   = idot(wI, vI);  wHw = idot(wI, Hw);  r2 = idot(r, r)
lam_min_lb = c + mu_lo_cert                           # rigorous lower bound on lambda_min(H')
corr = interval(0.0,  sup(r2)/lam_min_lb)             # [0, ||r||^2 / lambda_min]  =  defect enclosure
A2   = interval(2.0)*wv - wHw + corr                  # certified enclosure of <v, H'^{-1} v>

Note two rigor points: (i) the residual r and r2=||r||² are formed with the interval operator Hc, so they capture both the CG inexactness and the interval uncertainty of every matrix entry (the Coulomb moments are themselves certified intervals from the composite Gauss–Legendre quadrature); (ii) the CG solve uses the midpoint matrix only as a preconditioned guess — its inaccuracy does not threaten rigor, because any error re-enters through the enclosed defect term.

3. The remaining ingredient: a rigorous $\lambda_{\min}(H')$

The upper bound ($\dagger$) needs a rigorous lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of $H'=H+cI$:

$$ \lambda_{\min}(H') \;=\; c + \lambda_{\min}(H) \;\ge\; c + \mu_{\mathrm{lo}}, $$

where $\mu_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is a certified lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of the finite matrix $H$, taken as $\inf$ of the verified lehmann_behnke Galerkin enclosure of the sector ground ($\mu_{\mathrm{lo}}=\inf(\mu_1^{N'})$ in the code, passed as mu_lo_cert). With $c=1.0$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{lo}}\approx-0.55$, one has $\lambda_{\min}(H')\ge 0.45>0$, and the assertion lam_min_lb > 0 in the code verifies SPD-ness at run time. This closes the chain: every quantity in ($\star$)–($\dagger$) is either computed in interval arithmetic or bounded by a previously-certified interval.

4. Why the enclosed $A_2$ still gives a rigorous lower bound

The single-test-vector LG bound is the Möbius transform (with separator $\rho$, shifted $\rho'=\rho+c$)

$$ A=A_0-\rho',\quad B=A_0-2\rho'+\rho'^2 A_2,\quad \nu=A/B,\qquad \widehat\lambda \;=\; \rho' - \frac{\rho'}{1-\nu}, \qquad \lambda \;\ge\; \widehat\lambda - c, $$

valid iff $\inf(B)>0$ and $\sup(\nu)<1$ (both checked per run). The map is monotone in $A_2$ in the region used, so replacing the exact $A_2$ by its certified enclosure and evaluating the whole Möbius transform in interval arithmetic yields an interval $[\inf(\widehat\lambda-c),\ \sup(\widehat\lambda-c)]$ that provably contains the true LG bound; we report the infimum as the certified eigenvalue lower bound. The defect term entering through the upper end of the $A_2$ interval can only move the reported infimum in the safe (downward) direction, so A4-inexactness never inflates the bound.

5. Magnitude of the defect correction in the actual runs

Because CG drives the residual to $\lVert r\rVert\sim10^{-13}$, the defect enclosure width $\lVert r\rVert^2/\lambda_{\min}$ is of order $10^{-24}$ — many orders below the $A_2$ interval width ($\sim10^{-10}$, itself set by interval rounding across the $D\!\sim\!35\text{–}69\text{k}$ matvecs). So the A4/auxiliary step contributes negligibly to the final width while remaining fully rigorous.

6. Summary of the answer to “how is A4 validated?”

  1. A4 asks for $w=H'^{-1}v$ exactly; with the Coulomb $1/|x-a_i|$ term this is impossible in a finite basis.
  2. We compute $\tilde w\approx H'^{-1}v$ by float CG on the midpoint matrix (accuracy irrelevant to rigor).
  3. The exact quantity needed, $A_2=\langle v,H'^{-1}v\rangle$, is written by the Goerisch defect identity ($\star$) as computable terms plus the single defect energy $\langle r,H'^{-1}r\rangle$, $r=v-H'\tilde w$.
  4. The defect energy — the exact “shift part’’ of $w$ that A4 would still require — is rigorously enclosed by $[0,\ \lVert r\rVert^2/\lambda_{\min}(H')]$, using $\lambda_{\min}(H')\ge c+\mu_{\mathrm{lo}}$ from the certified Galerkin ground.
  5. The whole LG Möbius transform is evaluated in interval arithmetic; monotonicity in $A_2$ guarantees the reported infimum is a rigorous lower bound. A4 is thus satisfied in the enclosure sense, not pointwise — which is exactly what a verified lower bound requires.
